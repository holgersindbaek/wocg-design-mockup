Collage studies

Round three: the tiles pack tight, like one composed image. One 12px gap everywhere — the tile-setters’ band (a gap must be at least 3× your worst edge wobble, and 2–8% of the module’s short side), below the 16px line where tiles stop reading as separate cards. Five packings, each from a documented tradition: quilt blocks, Hockney’s joiners, justified photo galleries, brickwork, and the framer’s pinwheel hang.

U1 · The twin pinwheels

The quilt whirligig and the framer’s pinwheel hang: four tiles rotate a quarter turn around a small square void, so the block spins without anything tilting. Two blocks side by side, a suit resting in each eye. Rotational symmetry is the one order the eye reads instantly but almost never sees on the web.

U2 · The courthouse steps

The quilt block built for wide frames: strips added in opposite pairs around a centre — and here the centre of the quilt is the text itself. Every seam is 12px, every strip a tile. The words live inside the pattern instead of beside it.

U3 · The joiner

Hockney’s composite polaroids: a strict 3×3 grid where every frame misses its slot by a few pixels and a degree or two. The grid stays reconstructible, so the deviation reads as life, not error. Jitter is deterministic and capped at half the gap; hover a tile and it straightens.

U4 · The contact sheet

The justified gallery (Flickr, Google Photos): every row one height, both edges laser-flush, the tile ratio solved so the block fills its box exactly. Two rows of three small, one row of two large — hierarchy by row, not by scatter. The most photographic of the five.

U5 · The brick wall

Running bond, laid like a real wall: the offset row closes with half-width headers at each end, so every edge stays dead flush — no bleed, no ragged ends. The 12px gap plays the grout. The most nostalgic packing here, and the only one that feels load-bearing.
