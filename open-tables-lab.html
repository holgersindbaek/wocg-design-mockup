Open tables lab · what a table tile must say

The felt top of the open-table tile works: you see who is at the table. The white bottom does not: it says the game name and repeats the seat count on the button. This lab first settles what a tile must say (from the listing payload, the production modal, competitor lobbies and three player lenses), then tries bottom panels that say it. Every section shows the same sample tables so the states compare: waiting, in play with a bot seat, ranked, a friend's named table with a rule variant, two and three seat games, a table with entry limits, and the placeholders. Three rounds were built and judged; the page keeps the seven tiles that still matter (four open, three folded) and the verdicts of every round. The removed sections live in the git history up to commit dfbb922.

Zero · the brief: what a tile must say

Settled from the listing payload (Venue.js getTableListings), the two production tile builders, thirteen competitor lobbies, thirty days of listing analytics and three player lenses (the returning regular, the cautious newcomer, the host and their friends). The decision a tile serves is one question: do I sit here? Everything ranks by how much it moves that answer.

Vital every tile, at a glance

  • Who is seated. Faces, names, bot badge, liked star, finisher badge. The felt does this already. Keep it.
  • Waiting or in play, in words. "Waiting" or "In play · 12 min", and above all "a player left, the seat is open". The one fact the felt cannot show. A fresh table and a 40 minute old game with an open seat look identical today. Never a seat count, the felt shows those.
  • The game, on the homepage grid. In the See all box the filter names the game (99.7% of use is one game), so there the state takes the bold slot.
  • One verb on one button. Join, Take the bot's seat, Host table. The button is read as the promise. A seat count is not a verb.
  • What a bot seat means. You take the bot's hand and its score, mid trick. Every listed table lets you do it, and nothing warns you today.

Need to have on the tile, quiet or conditional

  • Ranked as one quiet word. Private with the saved table name as the title.
  • The rule variant, only when it differs from the site default: Tournament rules, Dealer may pass, No bag penalty, Hearts on first trick. Stick the dealer, Bidder out and Seventh card are the defaults; naming them says nothing.
  • "Starts when you sit" when one seat is missing.
  • No bots when the table waits for real people. Bots allowed is the default and worth no ink.
  • Experienced only, registered only as one quiet phrase. The server lists only tables you may join, so these describe the company, never a gate.
  • Partner colours on the felt, and a seat hover that names the partner you would get.
  • Friend tables first and marked. The server sorts them first; the client throws that away today.

Nice to have hover, tooltip, See all

  • Elo, games, finishes per player, in the hover card (exists).
  • Target score or length: to 100, to 500, 4 deals. Already in the browser via C.js.
  • Slower turns on named private tables (2x, inferred from the name).
  • Waiting since 4 min on tables that have not started. Needs the created time on the payload, one line.
  • Rematch, host name, a quiet new under players with fewer than 10 games. Small server changes each.

Drop noise

  • Table number. Reused per game, means nothing across sessions.
  • The grey checklist. Six to eight rows, one or two active, in jargon. Nobody else does this.
  • Registered only, Bots allowed, Private table as standing rows.
  • The seat count in the button. The felt and the state line already carry it.
  • "0 min elapsed" on waiting tables, and "a looong time" (tables die at 4 hours).
  • Elo numbers on the face. A scoreboard, not company.
  • Scores, hand numbers, spectators, Watch. Not sent, or no such concept exists.
  • Reputation score. A private moderation signal. Never.

Round two rulings Holger, after round one

  • No seat counts. The felt already shows the empty seats. "Waiting for 2 more" says nothing the faces do not.
  • The state that matters most is a game in play where a player has left and the seat is open again (state j). Say that before anything else.
  • Facts may sit on the felt, but not the game name. The time gets one fixed slot on every tile.
  • The finish badge stays on the player, as production does it: the green completion badge at 70% and above with ten finished games, gold at 100%.
  • Seats are how you join. The new frontpage already lets you click an empty or bot seat. Keep that. A separate Join button is open to question.
  • The less important facts go in a tooltip, not a drawer, and never the player stats, which the seats already carry.
  • Not wordy. No sentences, no game icon, no colour strip, no ticket dressing, no centre placard. A status plate in the nameplate grammar is welcome.
  • Try four across and try the whole tile as one table.

Round three rulings Holger, after round two

  • One big table is the direction (c17), but the whole card must be 4:3, the felt's own shape. The footer line lives inside the felt and takes less room: the game name at the left, something else at the right.
  • The clock plate of Four across stays (top right, "10 min"), but in another colour, yellow. Before the deal it says "Waiting", or something better.
  • What is vital besides the clock and Waiting. Who is seated (the felt). A seat that opened mid game, said loudest. The game, on the mixed grid. Ranked or casual, one word. The rule variant only when it differs. The seats as the way in, and what a bot seat means. Everything else is a tooltip.
  • Round one and two still hold. No seat counts, no sentences, no game icon, no colour strip, no centre placard, the finish badge on the player, the game name never as a plate in the felt's top corners.

Facts that shape the copy. The open-tables box shows 0 to 5 tables 77% of the time and 1 to 3 tables half the time, so each tile has room to speak. Euchre, Spades, Hearts and Double Deck Pinochle carry 80% of listing use, so partnership games and their options come first. Bots are invited by a seated player, not by a timer, so a tile may never promise a start time. A ranked table stops listing as ranked the moment an original player leaves, so an in-play table with a bot seat is always casual for the joiner. Guests are "Guest #4,821". Five and six seat Go Fish tables cannot draw seats 5 and 6 today.

The one idea everyone agreed on. The state belongs on the felt. A small paper placard in the empty middle of the table (Waiting for 2 more, In play · 12 min, Starts when you sit) is read in the same glance as the faces, so a row of three answers "do I sit here" before the eye reaches the white part. Every judge put c11 in their top three, and the mean scores that rank c1 and c7 above it reward safety and completeness, not the idea. The split is only about what sits under the placard: the regular and the newcomer want c1's bottom (bold game name, a quiet mode word at the right, one facts line, one full width verb), the homepage composer and the craft critic want c12's one row (identity over the facts line, a small verb at the right) because it lands the tile at 276px next to 296px game tiles. Both were built: c13 and c14. The synthesis judge rates both above c11 and gives c14 the edge on scanability and height, c13 the edge on the verb (the full width button and the long honest label).

The grafts that landed in both syntheses. c1's quiet mode word with a plain sentence on hover for every jargon phrase. c7's priority ordered facts line that drops whole facts from the end into an ellipsis with a tooltip. c10's friend first line and verb (BobK is here, Join BobK) with a warm tint. c3's placeholder with your own chair pulled out (Your seat). c4's seat plates that roll to Sit here and Take over, with the partner named in the seat tooltip. The bot named in the fact line (You take Bot Ada's hand and score). Left on the table on purpose: c2's big tabular number, c7's colour band (a 3px colour edge on the bottom panel would be the quiet compromise if the mixed grid needs it), c9's drawer, and c8's row, which is the right shape for the See all box on a phone.

Before it goes into the mockup

  • One verb set, written once. Join, Join BobK, Take the bot's seat, Take a bot's seat, Host table, Host ranked table. Host buttons in the green chrome.
  • A width guard on the placard. About 106px on the 4 seat felt, ellipsis, short fallbacks (Last seat, 2 more), checked with Verdana forced and on the 2, 3, 5 and 6 seat felts.
  • No ink for defaults. No "standard rules" filler, no Bots allowed, no Registered only as a standing row. The variant shows on in-play tiles too.
  • A tooltip on every folded line and every quiet phrase. One dictionary, one plain sentence each, checked against the rules pages. No "you qualify", no "10 finished games", no "slower turns".
  • A touch path. The tooltip engine is hover only. Anything hover only needs a tap target or a second line on phones.
  • House chrome only. The 32px button or the 24px small button, never a third size. Typographic apostrophes. Nothing under 12px.

What the homepage needs around it

  • A phone rule for the grid. One column at or under 480px, two to 860px. The mockup has none and the current tiles break at 360px.
  • The New table card at the same height. Today its body stretches every tile in its row to 381px. Match it, or set align-items: start on the grid.
  • Friend tables first. The server sorts liked players' tables first and the client re-sorts by age. Keep the server order.
  • Dark canvas. 24% of views are dark mode. The placard is paper and the felt is felt, so both survive. The white bottom needs a dark twin.
  • The See all box. The placard stays, the identity line goes, and c8's row form is the candidate for phones.

What the payload needs

  • Nothing, for the tile. Every fact on c13 and c14 is derivable from today's table.listings payload plus C.js.
  • A default map for the e[] codes. Stick the dealer, Bidder out, Seventh card and standard Hearts are the defaults, so the fact line prints only what differs.
  • replaceRobots is true on every listed table, so "Take over" is honest today. Send it anyway so the client never guesses.
  • Optional, small server changes. The host name (from the earliest joinedDate) for "BobK's table". The created time for "waiting since 4 min". The raw start timestamp so the age stops double rounding.
  • Bugs found on the way. Five and six seat Go Fish felts draw phantom seats. Post game chat rooms list as "Table undefined". Table ids are used as CSS selectors and one in six starts with a digit. Reserved seats draw as takeable. Bot seats are only clickable when the table is full.

Verdict · round three

Round three built the one big table at a strict 4:3 in three ways, all with the gold clock plate at the top right that reads Waiting before the deal and the minutes in play after it, and a green Seat open plate under it on the table a player left. The judge pass could not run: the subagent service was overloaded on five launches in a row, so the three variants were built by hand and this verdict is the orchestrator's own reading, not a panel's.

The footer. The corners keep the felt exactly as production draws it, but 80px is too little for the facts the brief marks need to have. The rail gives the footer a full line, which is where the game name and the one right hand phrase belong. c21 pays for it with 7px of each face; c24 pays with 32px of tile height and keeps the felt whole, which is the trade Holger chose. So c25, the last refinement of c24, is the recommended shape: game name at the left, and at the right the people first (BobK is here, Take over Bot Ada) or else the one quiet fact (Ranked, Tournament rules, Last seat, No bots), with the whole list in the tooltip.

The checklist. Production lists Registered only, Experienced only, Private table, Bots allowed and the game's rule options on every tile, ticked or grey. None of it is dropped here, it is sorted. A row that is on and differs from the default is news and goes on the rail (Private, No bots, Tournament rules, Experienced only). A row that is at its default says nothing about this table (Bots allowed is the default on a casual table, Stick the dealer is the default in Euchre, Registered only is forced on every ranked table), so it waits in the rail's tooltip, where c24 shows the whole checklist exactly as production does, ticks and all.

What else is vital, besides the clock. From the brief, and unchanged by three rounds: who is seated (the felt carries it), the game name on the mixed homepage grid, what a bot seat means (the seat rolls to Take over), and a way in (the seats). Then the quiet need to haves, one word each and only when they apply: Ranked, Private with the saved name, the rule variant when it differs from the default, Last seat when the deal starts as you sit, No bots, and the entry limit. Everything else, the target score, who may sit, the host, is tooltip material.

Four across. Only if the grid can be 1112px wide. At the homepage's 902px next to the ad rail the tile is 213px and the plates and names clip, so three across at 290px stays.
