Scatter studies

Ten arrangements of the scatter, each borrowed from a documented composition device — painting armatures on one side, measured tile-grid practice (Poki: one pitch, one 16px gap, three sizes, zero rotation) on the other. Every tile is fully inside the frame, every gap comes from one scale, and no edge nearly-aligns: it aligns or it clearly does not.

T1 · The rabatment

The painter’s square-within-the-rectangle (Rembrandt, Monet): the frame folds into two implied squares. Text fills the left square, a one-pitch mosaic fills the right square exactly, and the strip between them stays empty as the seam.

T2 · The Hokusai band

Hokusai’s own stated rule (mitsuwari, 1814): two bands of sky, one of earth. The tiles form a ragged skyline along the bottom band — flat base, equal 16px gaps, varied heights — the upper two-thirds stays open except the text and one small “guest” tile floating alone, like Fuji behind the wave.

T3 · The steelyard

The atelier counterweight rule: a large mass near the fulcrum balances a small mass far out on the other arm. The seamed cluster sits just right of centre; one isolated small tile, far left and low, holds the whole frame level. Isolation makes it heavy.

T4 · The pyramid

Raphael’s stable triangle: tallest tile at the apex line, neighbours stepping down symmetrically, every bottom on one baseline, every gap 16px. The tile group’s silhouette is the pyramid; the text crowns it.

T5 · The Mondrian

De Stijl partition: seams run edge to edge, rectangles are unequal, and balance comes from counterweighting — one big quiet field (the text), one dominant tile, and a small saturated counter in the far corner. Nothing floats; everything is a cell of the frame.

T6 · The phi stair

The cascade, rebuilt with no overlap: five tiles step down one diagonal, each 0.786× the last (the golden root), side gaps a constant 16px, every tile whole. The slope frees the lower-right corner and the text takes it — the mirror of the usual reading order, which is what makes it feel composed rather than templated.

T7 · The Arnheim ring

Arnheim’s structural net: a rectangle’s hotspots are its centre and the four points midway to each corner. The text holds the centre; four tiles park exactly on the mid-diagonal points; two smalls extend the ring at the horizontal extremes. Radial order without literal symmetry — the hero tile breaks it, so the eye starts there.

T8 · The arc

Good continuation, Tiepolo’s device: one size of tile, equal 16px gaps, centres riding a single shallow curve — the dealt spread without the rotation. The purest Yahoo-style equality in the set; the curve alone keeps it from being a toolbar.

T9 · The snapped scatter

The original scatter, disciplined by the broken-grid rule: it only looks free. Three exact sizes, every position on the lattice, three shared alignment lines (one top, one left, one right), and gaps drawn from a scale of 16 and 48 — same or clearly different, never almost.

T10 · The one-corner stair

Ma Yuan’s one-corner school: all the mass packed into the lower-right corner as one seamed staircase — every gap 16px, bottoms and rights on shared lines — while most of the frame stays open. One small guest tile sits deep in the emptiness, the way Southern Song painters placed a lone boat.
