Ways into the tutorial

20 Oct 2026. Every game has an interactive tutorial with the Professor. Today a new player cannot find it in the app. This lab shows where it is today, then 18 ways to lead players into it.

The phones are the Canasta app on an iPhone Air (420 by 912), screenshots of dev in WebKit. The new parts are drawn on top in the app's own styles. Press ☆ Pick on the ones you like. The bar at the foot collects them for you to copy.

My pick: four together, then measure

  1. B8, a tutorial card at the top of the How to play box, with the link fixed so the tutorial starts in the app. This is the fix for today's gap. Small to build.
  2. B1, a card for new players on the home, under the doors, until the first finished game. Most new players see the home first.
  3. C4, a Tutorial button in the Table panel, beside Rules. A player who is lost mid-game finds it where Rules is.
  4. C2, the hint lamp offers the tutorial when a new player is stuck. This catches the players who need it most, at the moment they need it.

Then add the events: TutorialStarted (with the place it came from), TutorialFinished and TutorialExited (with the step). After two weeks we can see which place brings players in. We can also see if they play a second game more often.

A1 · First open

Ask once: do you know the game?

A sheet after the launch, one time per app. “No” starts the tutorial. “A little” plays a game with the hints on. “Yes” goes to the home.

Good
Every new player sees it. The answer also tells us who is new, so the hints can follow it.
Risk
One more tap before the first hand. Cribbage Pro asks the same; most apps in the onboarding lab ask nothing.
Build
Medium: a sheet, one saved answer, the hints set from it.

A2 · First open

A third door: Learn

For a new player the doors get a third choice beside Join table and Play solo. It goes away after the first finished game, or stays as a lighter door.

Good
The strongest place on the home. It says “you can learn here” before the player picks.
Risk
Three doors are tight at 420 wide (the words drop to 21 px). It takes taps from Play solo.
Build
Small: one more door, a “new player” test.

C1 · First solo game

Ask before the first deal

The first time a player presses Play solo, the table asks before it deals. “Learn first” turns the table into the tutorial. “Just play” deals.

Good
It asks at the moment the player shows they want to play, not on the home.
Risk
It stops the first game. If most players press “Just play”, it is only a delay.
Build
Small to medium: the prompt, the switch to the tutorial table.

C2 · When stuckMy pick

The hint lamp offers the tutorial

When a new player is stuck, the lamp lights up and asks. Stuck can mean two moves the game refused, or the third automatic hint in one game.

Good
It finds the players who need help, at the moment they need it. Hint is 91% guests. In Rummy it is pressed 81 times per 100 game starts.
Risk
A wrong signal shows it to players who only think slowly. It must show at most once a game.
Build
Medium: the signal, the bubble on the lamp.

C3 · A refused move

A link in the game's own messages

When the game refuses a move, its message ends with “Learn the rules”. The words above are the real message from Canasta.

Good
It comes exactly when a rule is not clear. No new screen.
Risk
The tutorial starts from its beginning, not at this rule. To jump to the rule needs lessons (B4).
Build
Small for a link to the tutorial. Large for a link to the rule.

C4 · Table panelMy pick

A Tutorial button beside Rules

The Table panel (i) gets Tutorial next to Rules. Leaderboard and Report bug share the second row.

Good

It leaves the game. It must ask first at a table with people, as the leave warning does.

D1 · Game over

An offer on the game over card

For a player in their first games who lost, the end card gets a line under Play again. A winner gets nothing, or “Want to learn the finer points?”.

Good
A loss is a good moment to learn. It never stops a game.
Risk
Only players who finish a game see it. Players who quit in the first hand never get here.
Build
Small. The phone is the visual sweep's 390 by 664 shot.
D2 · The tutorial's end

Lead out of the tutorial into a real game

Today Canasta's tutorial ends with “The first team to reach 5,000 points wins. Good luck!”. The player must then find Exit. Instead, end on a card: “You know the basics. Play a real game?” with Play solo (hints on) and Back to the home.

Good
The tutorial only helps if the player then plays. This makes the next step one tap.
Risk
None that I can see.
Build
Small: one card at the end of Tutorial.js for all 18 games.

E. Outside the app

E1 · Web front page

Call the web menu's Rules “How to play”

On the website a game's menu has Rules. It already opens the How to play page, which starts the tutorial at once. Rename it “How to play”, or split it into Tutorial and Rules. The Table panel on the web gets the same Tutorial button as C4.

Good
Almost free. The page already gets 5,270 views a month from search.
Risk
A player who wants only the rules gets the tutorial first, as today.
Build
Small: FrontpageCatalog.js.
E2 · App Store

Say it in the store listing

One screenshot of the Professor teaching, with the line “New to Canasta? The Professor teaches you.” Players who do not know the game then know before they install that the app will teach them.

Good
It can bring in players who would not try a game they do not know.
Risk
It takes one of the ten screenshot places.
Build
Small: one screenshot per app.

Notes

  • The data: How to play page views from OpenPanel (screen_view, path contains /how-to-play-, last 30 days). Hint and Help use from September 2026 (hint-button-usage and help-button-intent).
  • From the code, not tested on a phone: the rules box's link is rules-fragment.dust line 11 (target="_blank"). The shell's shouldOverrideLoad only catches main-frame loads. Capacitor's createWebViewWith sends a new-window link to UIApplication.shared.open, which is Safari.
  • A study with 45,000 players (Andersen et al., CHI 2012) found tutorials helped most in the complex game (play time up by as much as 29%). In the two simple games they gave no clear gain. So a tutorial is worth more in Canasta, Hand & Foot, Pinochle, Bridge and Cribbage than in Go Fish or Old Maid.
