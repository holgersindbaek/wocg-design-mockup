Hearts: play online for free (solo & multiplayer)

Play Hearts online to your heart's content. You can pair up with people by joining one of our tables, play by yourself against our bots or create a private table and invite your friends and family to play. The game is free to play and is played online, so no download or registration is required.

Hearts is a 4-player trick-taking card game, much like Spades. However, unlike Spades, the game's objective is to take as few tricks as possible and avoid scoring points. Because of this, it's known as a game of evasion.

Rules

Here's everything you need to play your first game. If you want the full reference or prefer learning by doing, our guide on how to play Hearts has the complete rules and an interactive tutorial that walks you through a real game.

Objective

Hearts is a game of avoidance. Every heart card you take is worth 1 point and the Queen of Spades is worth 13. Points are bad. The game ends when someone reaches 100 points. The player with the lowest score wins.

The deal and the pass

Four players each get 13 cards from a standard 52-card deck. Aces are high. Before play starts, everyone picks 3 cards and passes them face down. You pass to the left on the first hand, to the right on the second, across on the third, and nobody passes on the fourth. Then the cycle repeats. Tables using tournament rules skip the no-pass hand.

The play

Whoever holds the 2 of Clubs leads it to start the first trick. Play goes clockwise and everyone must follow suit if they can. If you can't, you may discard any card, with one exception: no point cards on the first trick. Some tables run the "First-trick hearts" option, which lets a player with no clubs drop a heart on that first trick. When you browse tables, a green tick by "First-trick hearts" marks those tables. The highest card of the suit that was led wins the trick and the winner leads the next one.

You can't lead hearts until someone has "broken hearts" by discarding a heart (or the Queen of Spades) on an earlier trick. After that, hearts can be led like any other suit.

Scoring and winning

When all 13 tricks are played, everyone counts their points, 1 per heart and 13 for the Queen of Spades. If one player manages to take every point card, they've "shot the moon" and the other three players get 26 points each instead. Take all 13 tricks and you've "shot the sun", which hands everyone else 39. Hands are played until someone crosses 100 points. The lowest total wins the game.

How to win at Hearts

These tips come from our Hearts strategy guides and from real games on this site. The two big skills are passing the right cards and reading where the Queen of Spades will land.

Passing:

  • Pass your high cards first. Aces, Kings and Queens win the tricks you don't want.
  • Don't pass everything high, though. If you give away all your Aces, you can hand an opponent exactly what they need to shoot the moon.
  • If you have the Queen of Spades with 3 or more other spades, keep her. You can feed your low spades safely. With fewer, pass her on.
  • Passing the Queen of Spades to your right is fairly safe. Passing her to your left is asking to get her back on a trick you win.
  • If you hold fewer than 3 cards in a suit, consider passing them all. A void lets you dump dangerous cards the moment that suit is led.

Playing:

  • No points can be played on the first trick, so it's the one safe moment to burn your highest card.
  • Play high cards early in a hand, while opponents still have to follow suit.
  • Count the spades above the Queen and remember which high cards have been played. Once you know who holds her, you can play around her.
  • Aim your points at the "low man", the player with the lowest score. Dumping the Queen on whoever happens to be handy just helps the leader win.
  • If you sit to the left of the low man, don't lead spades. It lets them safely ditch the King or Ace of Spades instead of eating the Queen.
  • Sometimes you're last to play, the trick has points, and even your lowest card would win it. When that happens, throw your highest card of the suit. You take the same trick either way and get rid of a bigger risk.
  • Watch for a moon in progress. If one player is sweeping every point, take a heart early. One point to you beats 26 to everyone.

Frequently asked questions

What is "breaking hearts"?

Hearts are broken the first time someone plays a heart or the Queen of Spades. Until then you can't lead a heart. You can lead the Queen of Spades at any time, even before hearts are broken.

What does "shooting the moon" mean?

Shooting the moon means taking every point card in a hand, all 13 hearts plus the Queen of Spades. Instead of scoring 26 yourself, the other three players get 26 points each. If that still wouldn't put you in the lead, the game subtracts 26 from your score instead. It chooses for you automatically.

What is the difference between "shooting the moon" and "shooting the sun"?

Shooting the sun is a rarer and more difficult feat than shooting the moon. To shoot the sun, a player must take all the cards in a round, both point and non-point. If successful, the other players are penalized with 39 points.

How do I play Hearts with friends?

Host a private table and invite your friends. Click the fan of cards at the table to open the Play menu. If it shows "Multiplayer", click that first. Then choose "Host table", turn on "Private table" and type a table name. At your new table, click "Invite players" and copy the link for your friends. Friends can also choose "Join private table" in the same menu and type the same name.

Can I play Hearts against the computer?

Yes. This page starts you in a game against three bots right away, which is a good way to learn the game before facing live players. For another game, click the fan of cards to open the Play menu and choose "New game".

Can 2 people play Hearts?

Hearts is a 4-player game, so two people can't play it alone. You and a friend can share a private table and fill the two remaining seats with bots.

How long does a game of Hearts take?

About 25 minutes on average across the millions of games played on this site. A single hand takes a few minutes and games run until someone crosses 100 points.

Is Hearts free to play here?

Yes. Hearts is free in your browser with no download and no signup. You only need an account if you want to keep long-term stats or play ranked games.

What games are similar to Hearts?

If you enjoy Hearts, try the site's other trick-taking games, such as Spades, Euchre, Whist, Bridge and Pinochle.

Behind the games

There is always an open table on World of Card Games, at any hour of the day or night. Take a seat and say hi, or ask the bots to sit down with you. Playing is easy and free, stopping is the hard part.

I'm Holger, a designer and programmer from Denmark, and I look after the site on my own. It started back in 2011, long before I came along.

If something breaks, a rule looks wrong, or you'd like a game added, write to me at holger@worldofcardgames.com. I read every email myself and answer them all.

See you at the tables,

Real people right now

The numbers at the top of the page are live. You can see who's at every table before you sit down and join a game that's already under way.

You never wait

Pick a game and a table opens at once. If you'd rather play the bots, they sit down straight away and a solo table has no clock.

The table waits for you

If your connection drops in a ranked game, your seat is held for three minutes. A solo game waits for hours, so you can close the laptop and come back after dinner.

Help while you learn

For your first ten games of anything new, arrows point at a good card. Then they fade on their own. Every game has a rules page and a short tutorial.

Tables stay friendly

Mute or block anyone in one click and flag a message for review. People who keep spoiling games play the bots for a while.

Your own table

Give a private table a name and send your friends the link. They don't need an account to join and the same name works every week.

Frequently asked questions

Is it free?

Yes. All eighteen games are free to play and the ads pay for the site. If you'd rather play without ads, a small subscription turns them off and you see the price before you pay anything.

Do I need an account?

No. You can sit down and play as a guest right away. If you sign up, your name, stats, avatar and settings follow you to any computer, tablet or phone. Ranked games open after ten finished games.

Can I play on my own, right now?

Yes. Pick a game and choose Play solo from its Play menu. The bots sit down at once and a solo table has no clock, so take as long as you like over a hand.

How do I get to a table with real people?

Pick a game and choose Join table from its Play menu. If you're new, you first finish one game against the bots in that game. Then the tables with people open up for it. An invite link, a friend's table, a private table or a rematch skips that first game.

Can I play with friends?

Yes. None of them needs an account. Choose Host table, set Private table to On and give the table a name. Your friends choose Join private and type the same name. You can also send them the invite link from the table.

Can I play competitively?

Yes. Ranked games use a rating that starts at 1500 and moves with every result. There are daily, weekly and monthly leaderboards for each game. You need an account and ten finished games first.

I've never played this game. Can I learn it here?

Yes. Every game has a rules page and a short hands-on tutorial. In your first ten games of each game, an arrow points out a good move when you take a while. After that the Hint button shows one whenever you ask.

What happens if my internet drops mid-game?

In a ranked game your seat is held for three minutes, so you can come back to the same hand. At other tables with people it's held for a few seconds. A dropped connection isn't treated as quitting. A solo game waits for hours.

What if someone is rude at the table?

Hover over their name. On a phone or tablet, tap the three dots beside it. Choose Mute to hide their chat or Block so you're never seated together at a public table again. Each chat message has a Flag button. When two players at the table flag the same person, their chat is reviewed.

Are the cards dealt fairly?

Yes. My server shuffles the cards with a cryptographic random number generator before anyone sees a card. Only two kinds of game are set up on purpose. Your first solo game of each game replays the cards of a game that most players won. The daily challenge gives everyone the same cards that day. The shuffle tests are on the site.

How do I change a game's options?
Click Options under the game on the frontpage. Change what you like and save.
How do I find a table?
Click Tables under a game on the frontpage and choose Browse tables. Pick a table and click Join table. New players first finish one game against the bots in that game.
How do I play against the computer?
Click Practice under a game on the frontpage and choose Play bots. Opening a game from the top menu also seats you with bots.
How can I play with friends?
Click Tables under a game on the frontpage and choose Host table. Turn on Private table and give the table a name. Your friends choose Join private table under Tables and type the same name. At the table you can also open Info, click Invite players and send them the link.
How can I keep bots away from my table?
Click Options under the game on the frontpage, set Bots to No and save. You'll then be seated at tables without bots. These tables can take longer to fill. If a player leaves during a game, the others can agree to invite a bot.
Why should I sign up?
With an account you pick your own name. Your stats, avatar and settings follow you to any computer, tablet or phone. As a guest they stay in one browser. You also need an account to play ranked games.
How do I chat?
Click Chat in the bottom menu at the table. Type your message and press Enter. Solo games against the bots have no chat.
Someone is being rude. What can I do?
Hover over their name at the table. On a phone or tablet, tap the three dots beside it. Click Mute to hide their chat. Click Block and you won't be seated with them at a public table again. You can also click Flag on a message in the chat log.
What can I do if I accidentally blocked or muted someone?
Click Settings at the top right of the page and open the Account tab. Under People, open Blocked players or Muted players and click Remove next to the player.
I have another question. Who can I contact?
You can e-mail me, the owner of World of Card Games, your question directly at holger@worldofcardgames.com. I personally respond to every e-mail I get.
