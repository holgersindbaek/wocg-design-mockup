Saying what each section is, without adding noise

Every section on the frontpage is announced by two or three words sitting in a suit seam. A player who has been here before knows what they mean. A player who has not reads Open tables and has to work out from the tiles that those are real tables with a free seat, and reads Live tables and has to work out that those are other people’s games that you can watch. The two names are one word apart and mean different things.

The constraint is that the page has to stay as quiet as it is now. The frontpage carries seven of these titles, so a line under every one of them is seven lines, and the page stops being something you scan. So every variation below is judged on two things. What it costs in vertical space, measured on the stage and printed beside the name. And whether it survives a second visit, because a returning player should not be taught the same thing every time they come back.

Every variation draws the same three sections one under the other, at full size and full width, so the rhythm can be judged instead of one head in isolation. The three are Open tables, Live tables, and Trick-taking games, which stands in for the three catalog sections. Each table section shows one row rather than two, to keep this page walkable, and the seam runs to the edge of the column here rather than the edge of the window.

All stages

Zero · where this stands

The page as it runs now, and the same three heads with no seam under them.

One · a quiet line under the title

One short sentence per section. The family that works for everybody and costs the most, in six weights and positions.

Two · only where it earns its place

A row of game tiles explains itself. Four seats on green felt does not. So put the line only where a player has to guess.

Three · the title carries the meaning

Nothing is added to the page. The two or three words are simply chosen better.

Four · the line arrives on hover

The resting page stays exactly as it is now, and the section explains itself when it is pointed at. Hover the stages to see these two work. Standing still they are the page today, so the switch at the top draws the area each one listens to.

Five · a mark you can ask

A small question mark on the seam. Nothing is said until somebody asks for it. The two look the same at rest, so click a dot, or use the switch at the top to open the first one in each.

Six · the count already there

The bar under Open tables already prints a number. Give that number a few words and it explains the section for nothing. The live bar prints no number today, so that one has to be added.

Seven · once, not forever

Teach on the first visit and then stop, or teach only when the section is empty and has the room to talk.

Eight · one line for the whole page

Instead of a line per section, one line near the top that names them all.

Nine · the row says it, not the head

Leave the head alone and let the tiles do the explaining, either in a word on the tile or in a caption under the row.

Ten · a verb where a verb is natural

Holger read the verb titles in family three and said this. “I kind of like the verb titles. Except for the games sections. There they feel unnatural.” He is right about the games sections. A catalog heading names a kind of game, and a kind is a noun. So this family leaves all three catalog headings exactly as they are and turns only the two table sections into verbs. Every card says which searchable words leave the heading, because the site lives on search traffic, so a heading that drops the word tables pays for it somewhere.

Eleven · the hint that survives the pointer

Holger read the hint that comes from anywhere in the section and said this. “Hint on the whole section is interesting as well.” That card works, and the notes in that family list three faults. It blinks while the pointer travels, it does not exist on touch, and a newcomer has no reason to point at all. Each card below fixes one fault, so the fixes can be judged apart, and the last card stacks the ones that survive. Use the switch at the top to see them standing still.
