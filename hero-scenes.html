Hero scenes

Five ways the title, subtitle, capsule and the popular games become one composition — no “Popular games” heading anywhere. Built on measured field research: Poki’s secret lattice, Gumroad’s zero-rotation scatter, Puzzmo’s ragged columns, and real card-fan geometry. Every scene keeps one axis of chaos and three quiet alignments.

S1 · The bento hole

The text is not beside the tiles — it is a cell of the same lattice, so every edge of the prose block is also a tile edge and there is no seam to criticise. The capsule straddles the text cell’s boundary as the keystone. Hearts holds the big cell; nothing rotates.

S2 · The scatter

Gumroad’s discipline applied to Yahoo’s energy: sizes range five-fold but rotation stays at zero — one axis of chaos. One tile bleeds off the right edge so the field reads as a fragment of something larger, and exactly one small tile stitches into the text column, in front. The rest is air.

S3 · The hand

The one device nobody in the category uses: the games dealt as a held hand. A decoupled fan — rotation and lift like a fan (±7° ends, parabola rise), spacing like a grid, so tiles stay legible. Hearts holds the flat centre. Tiles deal in on load (460ms, 80ms stagger, firm departure, calm settle) — press Deal again to replay.

S4 · Ragged columns

Puzzmo’s newspaper device crossed with Tumblr’s cluster gaps: identical column widths, free tile heights, column tops deliberately staggered, and two gap values — 16px inside a cluster, 28px between clusters — so the tiles read as sorted hands. The calmest scene; the left edges are perfect, the tops are ragged, nothing else moves.

S5 · The table

The Card Room made literal: the type lives on the paper, the games lie on the felt — dealt with tiny ±2° settles and heavier shadows, three depth tiers, never more. The capsule sits at the table’s rail like a plaque on the wood. The room isn’t described; it’s shown.
