Avatar SVG lab · the capped man, edited in the file

One avatar, the classic ManCowboy.svg (a bearded man in a red backwards cap; the file's id is wrong), with the frontpage's outline (the four 1px #252525 drop-shadows) at 192, 96, 48 and 28px on the felt and on the paper. Every row is an edit to the SVG itself, not a coat over it: an inset border on a shape is a stroked copy of that shape clipped to itself, so the line lies inside the fill and the silhouette does not grow. The borders are 2 units in the 160-unit box (1.2px at 96, 0.6px at 48, 0.35px at 28), in a darker tone of the shape's own fill. Rows 8 to 14 try the other things the drawing can take: a deeper beard, a shadow under the cap, heavier feature strokes, a deeper shade side, a rule that borders every shape, and the comic version. Under each row: what changed, the contrast it buys (WCAG ratio of the border or fill against what it sits on), the file cost, and the risk.

What the file is

34 elements, all flat fills in 15 classes; no gradient; the shared ink #27273d draws the eyes, the eyelids, the brows and the zip tip. The parts, in draw order (later on top):

The cap is a crown #db1b1b with a darker left panel , a lighter right panel , a dark-red brim and three snapback dots. The face is one skin shape #fcc8bc with a shade band on its left third (1.17:1 against the skin: the light comes from the right). The beard is one shape in #d89c93, 1.55:1 against the skin, which is why he is clean-shaven at 48px. The shirt is three navy shapes (#32314b, a lighter centre ), 1.55:1 against the felt. The collar is three orange blobs #e88032 (2.9:1 on the felt) over a darker oval that is the shadow under the chin.

How the edit is made

For a shape P with fill F, two elements are added right after it in the file: a <clipPath> holding a copy of P, and a copy of P with fill="none" stroke="darker(F)" stroke-width="4" clip-path="url(#clip)". A stroke is centred on the edge, so clipping it to the shape leaves the inner 2 units. Nothing moves; the silhouette is unchanged; the outline coat still fits. The script that made these fifteen files is zz-tmp-build-avatar-svg.py; it targets the shapes by their index in draw order, so it is one avatar's script, not the set's.

<path id="Shape" class="cls-2" d="…"/>
<clipPath id="ib18"><path d="…"/></clipPath>
<path d="…" fill="none" stroke="#911616" stroke-width="4" stroke-linejoin="round" clip-path="url(#ib18)"/>

Cost. The file grows by the bordered shapes' path data: 19.4 KB today, 36.4 KB with the three borders (row 4), 44.4 KB for the works (row 11), 52.5 KB for the rule (row 12). Gzip halves the difference. Each avatar has a base SVG, three emotion SVGs and three Lottie JSONs: the SVG edit is a script pass over four files, targeting shapes by fill and size rather than by index; the Lottie side has no inset stroke (a stroke with a track matte per shape), so during the two seconds of a win, think or lose animation the face would show without its borders unless the Lottie files are re-exported with matched stroke layers. At 1.2px the flip is small; it is still a flip.

What else could be done to the drawing

  1. The beard first. It is the weakest part of the face: a pink 1.55:1 from the skin. The border (row 2) gives it an edge; the deeper fill (row 8) gives it a mass; both together (row 11) make him bearded at 28px. If only one thing is done, do this.
  2. The cap's seams. The border in the brim's own red (row 1) turns three flat red shapes into a sewn cap and sharpens the cap-to-forehead edge from 3.4 to 6:1. The dark line also survives the aged eye where the panels' 1.1:1 differences do not.
  3. A shadow under the cap (row 9): a 6-unit band at 13% black clipped to the face. The cap sits on the head instead of beside it. Subtle at 96, gone at 28; a 96px refinement.
  4. Heavier feature strokes (row 10): the brows and eyelids are 2.39 units, 0.7px at 48 and 0.4px at 28; at 3.2 they hold at 48. The eyes themselves are filled ovals and read at every size.
  5. A lit side and a shadow side (row 14): the face's shade band is 1.17:1, invisible under 96; one step deeper (1.32:1) gives the head volume at 48. Two steps would turn it into a stripe.
  6. The shirt on the felt is 1.55:1 and no border fixes an outer edge; that is the outline's job (the frontpage's line, or the keyline from the avatar lab). Lifting the navy one step () would help the felt and hurt the collar; leave it.
  7. The collar's blobs (row 6): a border in the darker orange separates the three; without it the collar is one mass at 48. Optional: it adds lines to the loudest part of the figure.
  8. The teeth are a white block with no upper lip line; a 1.5-unit ink line along the top would make a mouth of it. Not tried here; it is a drawing change, not a border.
  9. Not this: the comic version (row 13). Every major shape in the shared ink turns the flat pack into a line drawing; crisp at 28, heavy at 96, and a different style from the other 157 files.
  10. If this goes to the whole set: the rule (row 12) borders every filled shape in its own fill darkened 28%, which a script can do to 158 files. Its caveat is visible on this face: the shade band's edge becomes a line down the cheek, because a rule cannot tell a part from a shade. The script needs a skip list per pack (shade bands, blushes, highlights), or a rule that skips shapes whose fill is within 1.3:1 of the shape under them.
  11. Housekeeping while in the file: give the parts real ids (cap, beard, shirt) instead of Path-19, so later scripts target parts, not indices; and fix the id, since this file is not a cowboy.

The pick

First round: row 11 (the works) is the most improved face and row 8 (the three borders plus the deeper beard) the one to ship; but the borders read as drawn lines, which was not the ask. Second round: the separation without lines. Row 10 of the softer set (the cap's soft shadow on the forehead, the shirt's on the neck, the beard's edge shaded, the beard one step deeper) says "here the skin ends and the red begins" the way the packs already say it at the neck, and nothing in it is a contour. Row 6 (contact bands in each part's own darker tone, 2 units, plus the deeper beard) is the crisper alternative for the 48px listing, where a blurred shade is one pixel wide. Either keeps the frontpage's outline as it is.
