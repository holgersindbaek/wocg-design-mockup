Playing-count lab

The same Popular-games grid, twelve ways to say "341 playing". The first two are what exists today (the mockup's white pill and the live site's dark corner tab). The other ten look for the same information with a lighter touch. Hover the tiles — several variants only speak when asked.

What the research says

Verified across Steam, Roblox, chess.com, Discord, Twitch, YouTube, lichess, Pogo, CrazyGames, Poki and Board Game Arena: every subtle treatment lives in one of two registers: muted naked text under the art (chess.com renders "215,261 PLAYING" at 50% opacity with no container; Roblox and Discord use gray caption rows; Discord's tiny green dot carries the "live" meaning so the number stays gray) or a small translucent corner tag on the art (the YouTube duration badge and Twitch viewer tag). Steam uses a third trick: naked text where the status-green color alone means live. Notably, no major product hides the count until hover (hover is for previews), so variants 3, 6, 8 and 10 here are genuine experiments rather than proven patterns. And two guardrails: most direct card-game competitors (Pogo, CrazyGames, Poki, BGA) show no live counts at all, and both Roblox's tile change and NN/g's social-proof research warn that small numbers backfire, which is what variant 11 addresses.
