The rail panels are gone; each card below is one concept for the small unit that replaces them, shown with the real live capsule. Two — date-shaped objects (the leaf family)

The tear-off leaf's idea, explored: the challenge indicator as a piece of calendar. Every card keeps the rank tag beside it so the comparison isolates the date object. Objects that carry personal memory (the week strip, the punch card) disappear for signed-out visitors; bare-date objects stay, unstamped.

Three — the researched tear-off family

Sixteen concepts came out of the research into real tear-off calendars (Abreißkalender, De Druivelaar, Soviet blocks, himekuri) and daily-game UI; these are the ten worth eyes. They vary along four axes that compose: the completion gesture, the month's memory, what the page reveals, and what's printed on the front. Cards marked “click” have a playable gesture.

Four — the big month leaf: the challenge as a todo, the board as a status

D1 grown up: a calendar page large enough to carry two printed lines — the daily challenge as a checkboxed todo, the leaderboard as a daily status. Twelve designs came from four lenses (stationery, planner, ink, product) and went through three judges; these six survived. Two are the merges the judges asked for. All keep the family grammar: the band flips green, done content strikes at 45%, and the numeral takes the stamp.

Five — the red cap desk block
